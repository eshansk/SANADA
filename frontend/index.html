The Complexity Ceiling: Why Speech Separation Collapses at K > 4

Empirical benchmarking across the SANADA dataset suite uncovered a physical performance barrier in time-domain speech separation. While source counting and multi-label tagging scale accurately up to K = 32, Conv-TasNet separation degrades from +10.59 dB at K = 2 to -1.15 dB at K = 5, proving that mask-based separation becomes mathematically intractable when mixture density exceeds four concurrent acoustic sources.

Acoustic Mixture Synthesis Pipeline

Deterministic Seed 42

Four Foundational Synthesis Rules

1. Full 8.0-Second Continuous Overlap
Every constituent stem is padded or looped to span the full 128,000 samples, avoiding partial-overlap shortcut learning.
2. -3.0 dBFS Peak Anti-Clipping Attenuation
Composite mixtures are gain-attenuated to ensure true peak amplitude remains strictly below 0.0 dBFS across all 15,559 files.
3. Pre-Mix 70/15/15 Zero-Leakage Partition
Speakers and acoustic sound events are partitioned into Train/Validation/Test sets *before* mixing to prevent data contamination.
4. Deterministic Seed 42 Initialization
Pseudo-random generator states are fixed to guarantee 100% bit-exact mixture reproduction across distributed evaluation clusters.

The O(K!) Permutation Ambiguity Barrier

Separating unconstrained monaural mixtures requires matching each predicted audio stem with an unknown ground truth emitter. Utterance-level Permutation Invariant Training (uPIT) solves this by finding the optimal permutation amongst all K! assignments. At K = 4, there are 24 permutations; at K = 9, there are 362,880 permutations; at K = 15, there are over 1.3 trillion permutations. This factorial explosion, combined with overlapping spectrotemporal energy masks, creates an intractable complexity ceiling where gradient updates destabilize.

Permanent DOI: 10.21227/z7hv-e014 • Host: IEEE DataPort

SANADA addresses the physical impossibility of collecting real-world high-density acoustic recordings with isolated ground-truth stems. By synthesizing three domain-isolated corpora (Org-Mix-4, Org-Mix-9, and FSD-Mix) with pre-mix 70/15/15 zero-leakage partitions and -3.0 dBFS anti-clipping attenuation, it provides the first rigorous benchmark proving the Complexity Ceiling—the point (K > 4) where speech separation drops below 0 dB SI-SDR.

Machine Listening Architectural Glossary

SI-SDR
Scale-Invariant Signal-to-Distortion Ratio (in dB). The standard benchmark for measuring reconstruction fidelity between separated stems and ground truth targets, independent of overall scale variations.
mAP
Mean Average Precision across classes. Measures multi-label sound event tagging quality across varying classification thresholds, prioritizing precision on sparse acoustic events.
ASL
Asymmetric Loss. Dynamically shifts negative gradients away from easy background non-events, preventing classifier saturation on severe positive/negative label imbalances.
uPIT
Utterance Permutation Invariant Training. Computes loss over all K! speaker permutations and backpropagates through the minimal-loss pairing, solving the speaker label ambiguity problem.

Research Publication & Dataset Citation

Official citation reference, paper abstract, and open reproducibility links for the SANADA acoustic benchmark dataset.

Abstract

Preprint, not peer reviewed (SSRN)

Acoustic source counting, multi-label sound tagging, and source separation are traditionally evaluated on disparate corpora with differing mixture lengths, sampling rates, and overlap policies. SANADA introduces a unified synthetic benchmark comprising 15,559 eight-second mixtures across 13 partitions derived from LibriSpeech and FSD50K. By enforcing strict pre-mix 70/15/15 zero-leakage partitions and -3.0 dBFS anti-clipping attenuation, we systematically map the Complexity Ceiling—demonstrating that time-domain separation collapses below 0 dB SI-SDR when mixture density exceeds K = 4, whereas continuous counting and multi-label tagging scale robustly up to K = 32.

Methodological Limitations

  • ⚠
    Monaural Only: All mixtures are evaluated on single-channel recordings (16 kHz). Multi-channel spatial and interaural time difference (ITD/IPD) cues are deliberately omitted to evaluate pure monaural acoustic separation capabilities.
  • ⚠
    English Lecture Speech Domain: Org-Mix-4 and Org-Mix-9 partitions are synthesized using LibriSpeech read speech, which exhibits clean acoustic environments and standard English accents. Highly reverberant conversational speech may exhibit lower baseline separation.
  • ⚠
    Fixed 8.0-Second Duration Invariance: Models are evaluated on standardized 128,000-sample segments. Real-time streaming chunking requires additional dynamic buffer windowing.
